I utilized the R2D2 framework as the feature detection algorithm. 
From Fig.~\ref{fig:1} and Fig.~\ref{fig:2}, it can be observed that most keypoints are 
concentrated around visually distinctive regions of the image, such as object boundaries, 
textured surfaces, and high-contrast areas. The clustering of keypoints occurs because R2D2 
detects features across multiple scales, which can lead to overlapping detections when strong 
structures are present at several pyramid levels. In the following plots, the radius of 
each circle represents the detected scale of the corresponding keypoint, illustrating the multi-scale 
nature of the detector.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/Q1.1.1_scaled.png}
    \caption{30 keypoints with scaled radius}
    \label{fig:3}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/Q1.1.2_scaled.png}
    \caption{30 keypoints with scaled radius}
    \label{fig:4}
\end{figure}

The radius of each green circle represents the scale predicted by R2D2 for that keypoint, corresponding to the 
size of the image region that contributes to its descriptor. Larger circles indicate coarse-scale features 
that remain stable across viewpoint and blur changes, while smaller circles 
correspond to fine-scale texture details. The variation in circle sizes illustrates the multi-scale nature of 
the detector, allowing robust matching across different levels of zoom and scene detail.
Since the R2D2 framework natively provide "scores" for each keypoint, it's easy to identify which keypoints
are less reliable when we perform image matching in the later steps.
